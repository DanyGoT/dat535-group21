## Pilot failure and corrected retry — 2026-11-05

The baseline failed at **00:41 UTC**, after **23,422.592 s** (about 6 h 30 min), while
decoding SX. [Its exact run record](silver-2026-10-29-baseline-failed.json) preserves the
input audits and exception. The service stopped, the waiting week controller stopped,
and normal failure cleanup removed this run's staging. No published Silver/Gold day exists
from this attempt. Collectors were unaffected; the cgroup peak stayed about 4.00 GiB.

The cause was two SX `StartTime` values of `0001-01-01T00:00:00+00:53:28`, which underflow
Python's supported UTC range. The parser now flags that field and retains its record,
matching its other invalid-timestamp handling. A complete read-only SX check verified
5,947 situation versions and 1,439 controls from all 4,701 messages, with exactly two
invalid-start flags and no schema/conversion errors.

The baseline also measured substantial avoidable staging: 850,820,065 call versions and
26,055,055 journey versions across three days became **280,853,976 call versions and
8,647,458 journey versions** for the target day after resolution. The corrected decoder
applies the same explicit-day scope before call timestamp conversion and Spark row creation.
Unknown dates/IDs and unusable orders remain available to resolve/quarantine; controls and
source-contract checks remain intact. Small feeds now decode first. The retry must reproduce
those target-day version counts; performance improvement remains unproven until measured.

Before retrying, all **29 Silver tests** passed in 87.239 s and all **5 Gold tests** passed
in 23.166 s, serially on the VM under the shared lock with a 2 GiB driver. Their transient
test unit peaked at 1,890,889,728 bytes. The corrected pilot uses
`dat535-silver-validation-retry-20261029-20261105.service` with the same three-core CPU quota,
two local workers and 5 GiB whole-service cap. The failed week controller remains stopped;
no historical continuation starts until the corrected pilot passes.

An additional [real-message equivalence check](silver-et-scope-equivalence-2026-11-05.json)
sampled the first two data messages from each of 24 closed hours on all three input days:
144 messages across 14 codespaces, including four journey versions without an explicit date.
All 68 surviving journey versions and 2,175 call versions matched the unscoped decoder plus
the equivalent scope rule, field for field and in entity order. Its small CPU timings are
validation timings, not a forecast of full-day throughput.
